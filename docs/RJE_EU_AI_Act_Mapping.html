01 · Scope

What this document does, and does not, claim.

The EU AI Act (Regulation 2024/1689) establishes a risk-based framework for AI systems placed on the Union market. The high-risk regime in Title III places concrete documentation, oversight, and record-keeping obligations on providers and deployers of high-risk systems.

This document maps the specific outputs RJE produces onto the Articles where they support the obligations of a high-risk provider or deployer. It is positioned as documentation support material, not as a compliance certification.

The high-risk classification (Art. 6 and Annex III) is determined by the deployer against its own system and use case. RJE does not classify; it supports the documentation and oversight regime that follows from a classification the deployer has already made.

Boundary

RJE supports documentation and oversight obligations. Conformity assessment, CE marking, registration in the EU database, and the substantive determination of high-risk classification remain the provider's responsibility. RJE is neither a notified body nor a substitute for legal advice.

02 · Timeline

When the obligations bite.

The Act enters into force in stages. The dates below are the applicable-from dates published in the consolidated text.

  • 02 February 2025 Prohibitions and AI literacy

    Title II prohibitions and Article 4 AI-literacy obligations apply.

  • 02 August 2025 Governance, GPAI, penalties

    Title V (general-purpose AI), Article 99 penalties, and governance provisions apply.

  • 02 August 2026 High-risk regime applies

    Title III high-risk obligations (including Articles 9, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 27) apply. This is the date organisations using RJE are typically preparing for.

  • 02 August 2027 High-risk regime · embedded systems

    Extended application date for high-risk AI systems embedded in regulated products under Annex I.

Where each output meets each obligation.

The table maps the obligations of a high-risk provider or deployer onto the RJE outputs that contribute to each. Numbering follows the consolidated Act.

Art. 10 Data and data governance Cases are persisted with full lineage. Precedent retrieval is logged. The data underlying every decision can be reproduced from the case record. Inputs are versioned; precedent shifts are auditable.
Art. 11 Technical documentation (Annex IV) The Methodology document and this mapping describe the intended purpose, the inputs and outputs, the safeguards, and the human-oversight provisions. They contribute substantive material to the deployer's own Annex IV pack; they do not replace it.
Art. 12 Automatic record-keeping Every deliberation generates a structured record: framing report, vote distribution, expert analyses, decision rationale, DOCX. Records are persisted with full lineage and timestamps; retention and retrieval terms are set per tier at contracting.
Art. 13 Transparency and information to deployers The decision document is human-readable and discloses the five-perspective structure, the vote, the rationale, and any dissenting positions. The Methodology document gives the deployer a complete account of how outputs are produced.
Art. 14 Human oversight Halts route to human review by design. Confidence scores and vote distributions allow a reviewer to triage which decisions need closer attention. Dissenting expert analyses are preserved on record, supporting overrideability.

Art. 50 Transparency to natural persons The decision document is suitable for disclosure to affected parties under the appropriate scope. Operating constraints can be cited directly from the recorded analysis.
04 · What RJE does not cover

Obligations the deployer retains.

The following obligations remain entirely with the provider or deployer. RJE's output may support them indirectly but does not discharge them.

  • Art. 16 (provider obligations) and Annex IV (technical documentation) — preparing and maintaining the full technical-documentation pack for the deployer's own high-risk system. RJE is not the deployer's high-risk system; it is decision-support infrastructure used by it.
  • Art. 17 (quality management system) — the deployer's QMS for its own high-risk system.
  • Art. 19 to 22 (conformity assessment, CE marking, registration) — the provider's own conformity assessment and registration of its high-risk system in the EU database.
  • Art. 26(1) (use according to instructions) — deploying the high-risk system in accordance with its intended purpose.
  • Art. 72 (post-market monitoring) — ongoing monitoring of the deployer's own high-risk system in real-world use.

RJE's role is to make the parts of the regime that turn on structured, documented, defensible reasoning easier to satisfy.

05 · GDPR alignment

A note on data-protection obligations.

RJE is deployed in compliance contexts where GDPR obligations frequently overlap with AI Act obligations. The following points are useful to coordinate with the data-protection function.

Data residency

Production infrastructure is hosted in the European Union. Deployment options for the Enterprise tier are discussed at contracting.

Records of processing

The audit trail supports Art. 30 GDPR records-of-processing obligations on the deployer side. The case_id and timestamp on every decision are designed to be cross-referenced with the deployer's own ROPA entry.

Automated decision-making

Where deployments invoke Art. 22 GDPR (decisions producing legal or similarly significant effects), the decision document supports the data subject's right to obtain meaningful information about the logic involved. The five anonymised expert analyses and the decision rationale together describe the reasoning in human-readable form.

Subject-access requests

The DOCX record produced at the time of deliberation is suitable for use in support of subject-access responses. Retrieval, language handling, and cross-border arrangements are set per tier at contracting.
