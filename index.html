01 · Quickstart

Submit your first case in under five minutes.

Every API key is provisioned against a tier. Once issued, a single POST request returns a complete deliberation: a framing report, a reasoned decision, the five independent expert analyses, and a fully formatted DOCX. There is nothing else to install.

Early access

The endpoint is provisioned per organisation. Token issuance, IP allow-listing, and (where applicable) private precedent namespace setup happen at access request. Write to the address below to start the conversation.

1. Obtain a session token

API access is provisioned per organisation. Request access by writing to growth.business@llulllab.com with the tier you intend, the contracting entity, and the expected use case. You will receive a session token by return.

2. Send a case

The endpoint is POST https://api.llulllab.com/rje/v1/deliberate. The full request and response schema is in the API view. The minimum viable request carries a description, a domain, and a session token.

Tip

Write the description as the decision to be made, not the topic. "Should we approve a new staff scheduling algorithm given Article 22 of the GDPR?" deliberates better than "GDPR Article 22 review."

3. Read the response

The response is a single JSON object. The most useful fields, in order of how teams typically consume them:

  • decision.final_decision — the synthesised outcome, one of approved, rejected, modified.
  • decision.decision_rationale — the reasoning a committee would want to read first.
  • voting.vote_counts — the five-evaluator distribution; useful for confidence calibration.
  • expert_analyses — the per-evaluator reasoning, anonymised by design.
  • docx_base64 — the full audit-ready document, ready to archive.

4. Handle halts

Some cases return status: "halted" rather than completed. The internal safety screen flagged the case as carrying extreme risk patterns and refused to issue a decision. Halts are deliberate, are not billed, and should be surfaced to a human reviewer rather than retried.

Note

Retries on transient failures (HTTP 503) are safe and idempotent. Retries on a halted response will return halted again. The safety screen is by design not bypassable through the API surface.

02 · Methodology

How RJE actually arrives at a decision.

RJE evaluates each case from five distinct analytical perspectives, independently. The architecture is a six-stage computational pipeline. Every stage is auditable; every stage's output is persisted; every stage can be reproduced from the case record.

  1. Case framing

    Before deliberation begins, the system produces a preliminary analysis that defines what approval, rejection, and modification would concretely entail for the case at hand. This grounds the five evaluators in the same operational frame.

  2. Five-perspective evaluation

    Five independent evaluators consider the case in parallel. They cover institutional governance, legal compliance, normative ethics, practical feasibility, and fundamental rights. Each produces its own analysis, position, and reasoning. They do not see each other's output.

  3. Precedent grounding

    Every new case is evaluated in light of prior cases held in the precedent library. As the library grows, deliberation quality improves over time. Enterprise customers may maintain a private precedent namespace.

  4. Vote and synthesis

    The five positions are tallied. A confidence score is derived from the distribution. A synthesised decision rationale reconciles agreement and dissent without erasing either.

  5. Anti-Totalitarianism safety screen

    Every output passes through a dedicated safety mechanism that screens for extreme risk patterns: rights suppression, due-process erosion, disproportionate coercion. If the screen detects such patterns, the system halts and refuses to issue a decision. The safety mechanism is designed to err on the side of caution.

  6. Decision document

    The system synthesises a reasoned decision document including the framing report, the final decision, the rationale, the vote distribution, and the five anonymised expert analyses. The document is returned both as plain text and as a formatted DOCX suitable for filing in an audit trail.

What expert anonymity gives you

Expert identities are anonymised by design. EXPERT_01 through EXPERT_05 are stable per-case labels but carry no methodological metadata. This foregrounds the reasoning over the methodology, which matters for two reasons. First, internal committees evaluate decisions on their substance rather than on the institutional weight of a label. Second, the design discourages selective citation of "the evaluator that agreed with us."

What you can defend to a committee

The output structure is built to survive scrutiny: every decision carries a confidence score, a recorded vote distribution, a written rationale, and five independent analyses. Dissent is preserved on record rather than suppressed. The infrastructure underneath is proprietary and model-agnostic by design; what is exposed is the deliberation, not the apparatus.

03 · EU AI Act mapping

Where RJE supports your Article-level obligations.

The EU AI Act high-risk regime (Title III) places concrete documentation, oversight, and record-keeping obligations on providers and deployers of high-risk AI systems. The Act enters into force in stages; the high-risk provisions apply from August 2026.

RJE is positioned as documentation support infrastructure. It does not certify compliance and is not a substitute for a notified body. It produces the structured, defensible decision record that the Act expects to find when a regulator asks.

ArticleObligationHow RJE contributes
Art. 9 Risk management system Each deliberation produces a recorded analysis from a fundamental-rights perspective and an institutional-governance perspective. Halts surface extreme risk patterns automatically and become part of the risk register.
Art. 10 Data governance Cases are persisted with full lineage. Precedent retrieval is logged. The data underlying every decision can be reproduced from the case record.
Art. 12 Automatic record-keeping Every deliberation generates a structured record: framing report, vote distribution, expert analyses, decision rationale, DOCX. Records are immutable and timestamped.
Art. 13 Transparency to deployers The decision document is human-readable and discloses the five-perspective structure, the vote, the rationale, and any dissenting positions.
Art. 14 Human oversight Halts route to human review by design. Confidence scores and vote distributions allow a reviewer to triage which decisions need closer attention.
Art. 15 Accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity The precedent base evolves under version control. Outputs are reproducible from the case record. Authentication is per-session-token; transport is TLS 1.3.
Art. 27 Fundamental Rights Impact Assessment The fundamental-rights evaluation perspective produces written analysis on every case. Combined with the per-case audit trail, this supplies the substantive material the FRIA expects.
Art. 50 Transparency to natural persons The decision document is suitable for disclosure to affected parties under the appropriate scope. Operating constraints can be cited from the recorded analysis.
Scope

RJE supports documentation and oversight obligations. Conformity assessment, CE marking, and registration in the EU database remain the provider's responsibility. The Readiness Package bundles template documentation aligned to the Articles above.
